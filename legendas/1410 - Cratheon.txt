You'll see headlines saying surgical robots are now 80-something percent autonomous. Good, that number is real. But it's also misleading. Let me explain. In experiments, robots have completed most steps of certain procedures on their own. Impressive, real, worth celebrating. But here's what that percentage hides. The steps a robot can automate are predictable ones. The remaining part, the unexpected bleeding, the anatomy that doesn't match the textbook, the split-second judgment call, that's the hard part. And it's exactly the part that decides whether a patient lives or dies. Automating the easy 90% doesn't get you 90% of the way there. The last 10% is the whole game. So, when you hear mostly autonomous, translate it as the machine can handle the routine and the surgeon is still essential for everything that actually matters. That's not a limitation to hide. It's the reason surgery is safe. The hardest part of medicine is the human part. Medicine? Ask Cratheon.
